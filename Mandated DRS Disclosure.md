# I. Executive Summary

Pursuant to Rule of Practice 192 (17 C.F.R. § 201.192), I petition the Securities and Exchange Commission (the Commission or SEC) to initiate rulemaking to require public companies to disclose, in Forms 10-Q and 10-K, the number and percentage of outstanding equity securities held outside the Depository Trust Company (DTC) nominee Cede & Co. (non-Cede-owned or NCO shares).

This disclosure would modernize periodic reporting to reflect current custody realities and would improve investor protection and market transparency by providing standardized, issuer-reported information about (i) the portion of shares effectively unavailable for securities lending and margin within DTC custody and (ii) the resulting implications for liquidity assessment, float interpretation, and short-interest metrics.

Supporting analysis and a detailed implementation framework are provided in Exhibit A (peer-reviewed article attached).

# II. Nature of Petitioner’s Interest

WhyDRS.org is a volunteer-driven investor education and advocacy organization focused on the Direct Registration System (“DRS”) and share ownership transparency. WhyDRS provides educational and reference information about U.S. financial markets, publicly traded companies, and DRS, and supports household investors in understanding how securities are held and recorded.

WhyDRS has a direct interest in ensuring that material information about equity ownership structure is disclosed in a timely, standardized, and comparable manner through SEC periodic reporting. In particular, WhyDRS believes issuers should report the number and percentage of shares held outside DTC nominee Cede & Co. (“non-Cede-owned” shares), because this information is relevant to investors’ assessment of custody risk, share availability within the DTC system, and the integrity of market metrics that implicitly assume DTC-held supply.

WhyDRS also engages in public-interest advocacy by encouraging investor participation in SEC rulemaking processes and by providing tools and resources intended to help investors communicate with regulators and issuers regarding disclosure practices.

# III. Requested Commission Action

I respectfully request that the Commission propose and adopt amendments to Regulation S-K (and corresponding Form 10-K and Form 10-Q instructions) to require registrants to disclose, for each class of voting equity securities registered under Section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934:

*   Total shares outstanding as of a specified measurement date;
    
*   Total shares registered to Cede & Co. (DTC custody);
    
*   Total shares not registered to Cede & Co. (NCO shares), including total number of DRS shares and other registered shares, expressed as both share count and percent of outstanding; and
    
*   The number of record holders associated with the class, or a cross-reference to the existing Item 201 record-holder disclosure, with optional breakout categories described below.
    

  
  

This petition requests rulemaking of general applicability, not adjudication or enforcement action.

# IV. Proposed Rule Text (Substance)

## A. Amendment approach (recommended)

The Commission could implement this requirement by amending Regulation S-K Item 201(a)(2) (Market Information; Holders) to add a new instruction requiring disclosure of NCO share totals (and Cede totals) for each class of common equity. Item 201 is a natural location for market-structure holder information, including record-holder counts.

## B. Proposed definitional substance

For purposes of the new disclosure item, the Commission should define:

**Cede & Co. shares:** Shares registered in the name of Cede & Co. (or any successor DTC nominee) on the issuer’s register.

**Non-Cede-Owned (NCO) shares:** Shares of the class not registered in the name of Cede & Co., including shares directly registered in the name of the shareholder on the issuer’s records (book-entry DRS) and shares evidenced by certificated form, and including shares recorded outside DTC custody under issuer/transfer-agent administered plans where the underlying position is not held through Cede & Co.

## C. Model disclosure (registrant-facing template)

The Commission could provide model language such as:

_**Non-Cede-Owned Shares (NCO) Disclosure. As of \[quarter-end date\], there were \[X\] shares of \[Class\] common stock outstanding. Of those shares, \[A\] shares (\[B\]%) were registered in the name of Cede & Co. and \[C\] shares (\[D\]%) were not registered in the name of Cede & Co. (non-Cede-owned shares). The approximate number of holders of record of our \[Class\] common stock as of \[date\] was \[E\].**_

## D. Optional (but useful) category breakout

To improve comparability and reduce interpretive ambiguity, the Commission should permit or require an additional breakout of NCO shares into:

*   DRS (directly registered) shares;
    
*   Certificated shares; and
    
*   Issuer/transfer-agent plan shares held outside DTC custody (for example, DRIP/DSPP structures).
    

# V. Reasons for the Requested Rulemaking

## A. Investor protection and fair disclosure

NCO share information can be economically meaningful but is inconsistently disclosed and often practically inaccessible to ordinary investors. Standardized reporting in periodic filings would reduce information asymmetry and improve equal access to material ownership-structure data.

## B. Market transparency and metric accuracy (float, liquidity, short interest)

When a meaningful fraction of shares are held outside DTC custody, those shares are generally unavailable for margin and securities lending within the DTC system. Omission of NCO share totals can distort market participants’ interpretation of:

*   Effective tradable supply (float in the practical sense);
    
*   Liquidity and volatility risk; and
    
*   Short-interest ratios that depend on an assumed available-supply denominator.
    

## C. Administrative feasibility and low incremental cost

Issuers (through transfer agents) already maintain the register information needed to compute Cede totals and NCO totals. This petition seeks a standardized reporting field, not a new data collection regime, and is designed to minimize compliance burden.

## D. Consistency with existing SEC disclosure architecture

The SEC already mandates disclosure of ownership categories that meaningfully affect tradability and control (for example, insider or affiliate ownership). NCO shares can have similar market-liquidity effects when they comprise a significant fraction of outstanding shares. Aligning disclosure treatment would modernize SEC reporting consistent with the Commission’s investor-protection mission.

# VI. Economic Analysis and Effects (Summary)

This rulemaking would (i) improve data quality for investors, analysts, exchanges, and regulators by standardizing an issuer-verified ownership-structure metric; (ii) reduce uncertainty and rumor-driven volatility where market participants debate true float; and (iii) impose minimal incremental cost because the disclosure relies on existing transfer-agent ledger data.

# VII. Implementation and Structured Data (Recommended)

The Commission should require Inline XBRL tagging for the new NCO and Cede share fields to promote machine-readable comparability across issuers and time, enabling SEC staff and market participants to evaluate trends in DTC custody withdrawals and potential liquidity implications.

# VIII. Request for Rulemaking and Conclusion

For the reasons above, I respectfully request that the Commission initiate rulemaking to amend Regulation S-K (and corresponding Form 10-Q and Form 10-K instructions) to require registrants to disclose, for each class of voting equity, standardized totals for shares held by Cede & Co. and shares held outside Cede & Co. (non-Cede-owned shares), including share count and percentage of outstanding.

Please contact me if SEC staff would like additional technical detail regarding definitional standardization, transfer-agent reporting conventions, or structured data tagging.
